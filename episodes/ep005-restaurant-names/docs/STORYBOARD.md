## Game grammar (the whole episode is a guessing game)
- **The name board.** Every brand name is typeset in code (Fredoka, white on navy with a gold underline) and is
  built letter by letter, so the transformations are literal: letters slide, split, swap and merge.
  **No logos, no lookalike marks**; the signs in R4 are blank panels that we letter in our own type.
- **Guess cards.** Before each later reveal (Wendy's 1:16, Taco Bell 2:22, Starbucks 3:06) up to three gold-edged
  guess cards flip in while the narrator asks; the natural pause after the question (1.4–1.6 s) holds on them,
  then the wrong cards fold away and the answer card flips gold.
- **Our character** (R2, the established golden-sweater character) plays the guesser: she looks up at the blank
  menu board during guessing beats. Code adds a "?" bubble that turns into a ✓ at the reveal.
- **Founders** appear only as generic illustrated figures seen from behind (R3) or as name tags. No faces,
  no portraits, no archival look, no quotes, no dialogue.
- **Origin stamps.** Each reveal ends with a gold "ORIGIN" stamp that is collected into the closing recap.
- Every illustration of a past event carries a small **"Illustration"** tag (bottom-left).

## Accuracy guards on screen
- Arby's: "Leroy" and "Forrest" spelled as Inspire Brands spells them; "Boardman, Ohio · 1964".
- Wendy's: no invented family scene or dialogue; the five tags are blank (the page says "all five of his
  children's names" without listing them); the home/sign sequence is framed "IMAGINE".
- Taco Bell: "Downey, California · 1962 · first Taco Bell"; the bell is a generic icon.
- Starbucks: "Starbo" is labelled as a place name on an old map; never as a company or former name. The ship
  is the Pequod; Starbuck is the first mate (a character).
