## Sources
1. **Chalmers University of Technology, "Groundbreaking research in bone conduction technology."** 🔁
   https://www.chalmers.se/en/current/news/e2-groundbreaking-research-in-bone-conduction-technology/
   As found: your own voice sounds different on a recording because, while you speak, you also hear
   it through bone conduction, not only through the air from your mouth. Cited in the draft at L20, L31.
2. **The University of Tokyo, "Simple questions and UTokyo": why does a recording of my own voice
   sound strange?** (Institute of Industrial Science, 2023) 🔁
   https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/en/features/z1304_00237.html
   As found: your voice reaches your ears by two routes (through the air, and conducted through the
   head); a recording captures only the airborne part, which is also what listeners hear.
3. **Orepic, Kannape, Faivre & Blanke (2023), "Bone conduction facilitates self-other voice
   discrimination," *Royal Society Open Science*** (open access, PMC9929504). 🔁
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9929504/
   As found: bone conduction from speaking contributes to how we perceive our own voice; playback
   studies usually use air-conducted sound only; adding bone-conducted vibration improved self-other
   voice discrimination, so self-voice is a multisensory experience. Cited in the draft at L39.
4. **Imperial College London, "Scientists explain in more detail how we hear via bones in the
   skull"** (2014; Nature Communications study with the Max Planck Institute for Brain Research). 🔁
   https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/153374/scientists-explain-more-detail-hear-bones/
   As found: hearing can be driven by vibrations reaching the inner ear through bone, not only through
   air. Cited in the draft at L63. (The page also says bone conduction makes us perceive our voice
   "as deeper than it is". The narration doesn't make that claim, and the video won't either.)

## What the video deliberately does not do
- No pitch-shifting. The before/after demo uses the **same spoken take** with a subtle EQ change at
  matched perceived loudness, labelled "Illustration — not an exact simulation."
- No "your skull is a subwoofer" explanation, and no claim that recordings perfectly reproduce
  what others hear.
- No claim that repeated listening will make everyone like their voice, and no claim to reproduce
  what any viewer hears internally.
- No numbers or frequencies on screen. Anatomy is a simplified side-view head diagram drawn in
  code, labelled "Simplified illustration."
